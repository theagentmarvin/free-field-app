The model exists. Your field crew can’t use it.

On site, decisions are made all day in front of a PDF with no information: what’s printed is only lines. Everything the model holds — the current revision, the properties, what’s behind the wall — stayed in the office.

When the sheet is a PDF carrying the model’s information — properties in view, current revision, the building walkable before it’s built — the decision changes nature: no more guessing, just verifying. And deciding with information is always cheaper than demolishing.

And whoever opens the door charges for it: per seat or per project. Either way, access is tied to an invoice that renews, your record lives on someone else’s servers, and your improvements wait on the vendor’s calendar.

FreeField is the missing layer between your BIM ecosystem and the field: the real model, the smart sheet and the issue in one place. And that place is yours: your team deploys it on your cloud and shapes it to how you build — one professional with Claude Code is enough. Because here the tool adapts to the company, not the company to the tool.

Issues in context: the model and the sheet side by side.

Load the model with its PDF sheets already aligned — automatically from Revit (with the exporter, opening soon), or by hand in two clicks — and walk them in LinkView. Sign in to your own project and the team joins in: multiple projects, role-based access.

    Two clicks on the drawing and two on the model, and the sheet is calibrated to its storey. From then on it is a smart sheet: linked to the model, with its layers of BIM information — click any element on the PDF and read its properties. A “Scale: calibrated” badge confirms it; without calibration you see an amber badge — FreeField never guesses metres.

  3. 3

    Plan on the left, 3D on the right, linked both ways. Drag the “you are here” marker and the camera drops to eye height, 1.65 m. Walk with W, A, S, D. Overlap mode drapes the plan over the model with an opacity slider, so you can see where drawing and model disagree.

  4. 4

    Defect, RFI, snag, safety, info. Pin the issue to a 3D element or a spot on the sheet, with a photo, a deadline and an assignee, and click the pin later to fly back to it. Export from Revit. Arrive calibrated.

Export once: the IFC and the PDFs arrive in FreeField linked automatically — every sheet calibrated to its level.

The FreeField add-in for Revit exports the model and the sheets linked automatically: every sheet tied to its IFC level in a single pass. No manual pairing, no human error — the field opens exactly what the office issued. Add schedules, element outlines or the IFC4 Reference View if you need them.

The result is a single bundle (.ffbundle): the IFC and a smart PDF per sheet — with BIM metadata embedded and accessible: click any element and read its properties. Drop the bundle into FreeField and everything arrives calibrated to its level, ready for the field.

An Android app for the job site.

The web app runs in any browser. FreeField Field is the installable Android app for the job site: it runs on phones and on the rugged, affordable tablets of the field, with or without a signal.

Models are stored on the device: the building opens offline, with model filters, measurements and navigation. Issues — with their photos and notes — go to durable local storage. If the 3D viewer crashes, the app recovers and carries on where you were.

On the roadmap

Own the tool, not a licence.

Everything is MIT. No seats, no per-project licences, no locked modules. The costs are your own hosting and your team’s time, and the next section spells them out.

No seats, no per-project licences

Add the whole crew, the subs, the owner’s rep. Nothing counts heads, and no module is locked.

Your cloud

Each company runs its own Firebase project: Auth, Firestore, Storage, Functions and Hosting. When an admin publishes a building, the model, the sheets and their calibrations live in your project for the whole team, along with issues, projects and members. All in your cloud and nobody else’s.

What you pay for, and what you don’t.

What you don’t pay for

Seats, per-project licences, per-user fees, locked modules or a licence renewal. Foremen, field engineers and subcontractors can all use it.

What you do pay for

Your own hosting, in your own cloud account. Your team’s time to set it up, keep it current and adapt it to how you work. Someone in your company who owns it.

What you don’t get

A support contract or an SLA. FreeField is a young open-source project. You get the code, the self-hosting guide and a community, not a vendor on call.

Known limits today

Anything not published lives only in the browser that loaded it. Roles are Admin and Member. iOS is on the roadmap. The exporter repo is opening soon. The Android repo is opening soon. Revision compare arrives in the next release.

Who it fits

A contractor with someone who can run a small web deployment, or who will contract for one. It runs on a Firebase project you control, and demo mode needs no backend, so you can judge it before you commit anyone. If you need a support SLA, it is not the right fit yet.

Your math, not ours

Compare the seats you would want to cover, times what you pay per seat, against your hosting bill plus the engineering hours you would spend. The second number is the one you control.

Questions, answered plainly.

Is this ready for a real project?

It is a young project, and we say so. The live demo shows what works today. Because the code is MIT, you can keep running your own fork whatever happens to the project. Pilot it on one building before you rely on it.

Free, so what is the catch?

No company is selling anything. Your costs are hosting and your team’s time, and there is no support SLA. You own the roadmap, with an open community behind it.

Is self-hosting a burden?

It runs on infrastructure you already control, through your own Firebase project, and you don’t need to be a programmer to get it running: with a Claude Code licence and the self-hosting guidethe skills bundled in the repo, a professional on your team can do it. You can also try the whole app with no backend at all in demo mode.

Where do our models and drawings go?

IFC files convert in your browser and the source file stays on your machine. Sign-in and tasks use your own Firebase project, with email-link sign-in, project roles and immediate revocation. If an admin publishes the building, the converted model, the sheets and the calibrations are stored in your own Firebase (Firestore and Storage); otherwise they stay in the browser. We make no certification or audit claims.

Will our models survive?

If the building is published to your project, the model and sheets are backed up in your Firebase and every member downloads them again. Anything unpublished is a browser cache: it can be cleared, but you can always re-import the source file. The Android app adds durable offline storage.

Does it work offline on site?

In two layers. The web app queues field capture offline in any browser. The Android app adds durable model storage and crash recovery.

Can I use the Revit exporter or the Android app today?

Not the Revit exporter yet: its repo is opening soon. Bundle import is already in the web app, so a bundle from the exporter will open as soon as it ships.The Revit exporter is public, MIT, in its own repo. The Android app’s repo is opening soon too.The Android app is public, MIT, in its own repo. The web app, with IFC and PDF import, is available now.

What about iOS?

The web app runs in any browser. The installable app is Android because that is what most site phones and affordable rugged tablets run. iOS is on the roadmap.

Does it replace our document control?

No. FreeField is the field layer: the model, the plans and the issues. Keep your document control and scheduling tools.
